Building the Digital Infrastructure for Agriculture.

DIA Technologies Pvt. Ltd. proposes a pilot implementation of Agricultural Digital Infrastructure (ADI) in the horticulture sector of Jammu & Kashmir — a technology-neutral digital foundation that strengthens coordination, interoperability, and trusted information exchange across the post-harvest ecosystem.

Explore the Pilot Proposal See the DIA Framework

What this proposal offers

  • ✓ A technology-neutral digital infrastructure framework — Agricultural Digital Infrastructure (ADI).
  • ✓ A working reference implementation — PHAOS, the Post-Harvest Agriculture Operating System.
  • ✓ A focused, evidence-based pilot at the IGC Lassipora Controlled Atmosphere storage cluster, Pulwama.
  • ✓ A collaborative model that complements, rather than replaces, existing government systems.
  • ✓ A scalable pathway from a single cluster to a statewide digital ecosystem for horticulture.

The Opportunity

Jammu & Kashmir has the physical infrastructure. The next layer is digital.

Jammu & Kashmir is internationally recognized for premium apples, cherries, walnuts, almonds, pears, plums, apricots, saffron, and other temperate horticultural crops. Government investment has built Controlled Atmosphere (CA) storage, cold stores, pack houses, grading units, and logistics infrastructure — but digital coordination across the ecosystem remains fragmented.

The Journey of an Apple

A single apple travels from orchard to grading centres, pack houses, transport networks, CA storage, wholesale markets, retailers, and finally the consumer. Every participant generates valuable information — yet it remains fragmented across organizations. The challenge is not the movement of the apple; it is the movement of trusted information.

The DIA Framework

Six infrastructure domains, one shared digital foundation

Agricultural Digital Infrastructure (ADI) is not a standalone application — it is an enabling layer built from six complementary domains.

01

Foundation Infrastructure

Trusted digital identities, institutional and asset registries, and spatial mapping — the basic building blocks of interoperability.

02

Knowledge Infrastructure

A shared repository of commodity knowledge, standards, protocols, scientific research, and advisory resources.

03

Operational Infrastructure

Digital coordination of workflows and activities — home to PHAOS, the first reference implementation.

04

Connectivity & Trust Infrastructure

Secure information exchange, digital governance, authentication, authorization, and audit trails.

05

Intelligence Infrastructure

Analytics, dashboards, forecasting, decision support, and Artificial Intelligence for evidence-based decisions.

06

Innovation & Service Infrastructure

An open foundation for governments, universities, startups, and enterprises to build new services.

Pilot Proposal

IGC Lassipora, Pulwama — a real-world proving ground

More than 70 operational Controlled Atmosphere storage units, growers, transporters, and traders make IGC Lassipora an ideal cluster to demonstrate ecosystem-wide digital coordination.

Demonstrate

Agricultural Digital Infrastructure within a real-world horticultural ecosystem, using PHAOS as the operational platform.

Coordinate

Improve coordination among stakeholders while enabling trusted information exchange and respecting institutional autonomy.

Scale

Generate evidence for a scalable model — from a single cluster to a statewide digital ecosystem for horticulture.
