Part III · The DIA Framework

Agricultural Digital Infrastructure & PHAOS

Just as physical infrastructure enables the movement of goods, people, and services, digital infrastructure enables the secure movement of information, coordination, and knowledge across an ecosystem.

Chapter 6

Our Vision: Digital Infrastructure for Agriculture

DIA Technologies was founded with a clear and singular vision: to build the Digital Infrastructure for Agriculture. Physical infrastructure such as roads, electricity, telecommunications, and cold-chain facilities enables thousands of independent participants to operate more effectively without replacing their individual functions — digital infrastructure can play a similar role.

The Company views governments, research institutions, farmers, FPOs, cooperatives, cold storage operators, transporters, processors, exporters, financial institutions, and technology providers as partners in a common ecosystem. The objective is not to centralize operations but to enable collaboration through shared digital capabilities.

Chapter 8

The Multi-Layered Architecture of Agricultural Digital Infrastructure

Agricultural Digital Infrastructure (ADI) is a unified framework of six interconnected infrastructure domains. Each performs a distinct role while collectively forming a shared digital foundation for agriculture that is interoperable, resilient, secure, and scalable.

01

Foundation Infrastructure

Establishes the basic digital building blocks of the agricultural ecosystem — trusted digital identities, standardized registries, spatial references, and digital representations of people, organizations, assets, and locations. Without this foundation, interoperability cannot be achieved.

Key components: Digital identities · Institutional registries · Asset registries · Geographic information · Spatial mapping

02

Knowledge Infrastructure

Creates a shared repository of structured agricultural knowledge that can be reused across the ecosystem — commodity information, quality standards, scientific research, post-harvest protocols, and advisory resources.

Key components: Commodity knowledge · Standards & protocols · Scientific research · Best practices · Advisory resources

03

Operational Infrastructure

Enables the digital coordination of agricultural activities — workflows, operational processes, record management, and ecosystem-wide coordination throughout production, harvesting, storage, transportation, processing, marketing, and exports. PHAOS is the first reference implementation within this domain.

Key components: Operational workflows · Activity management · Digital processes · Operational records · Reference implementations

04

Connectivity & Trust Infrastructure

Provides the interoperability mechanisms that enable trusted collaboration across the ecosystem — secure information exchange, governance mechanisms, authentication, authorization, auditability, and digital trust.

Key components: Interoperability · Information exchange · Digital governance · Authentication · Authorization · Security · Audit trails

05

Intelligence Infrastructure

Transforms information into actionable intelligence — analytics, forecasting, decision support, Artificial Intelligence, and performance insights that assist governments, institutions, enterprises, and farmers.

Key components: Analytics · Dashboards · Decision support · Forecasting · Artificial Intelligence · Performance insights

06

Innovation & Service Infrastructure

Creates an open foundation upon which governments, universities, startups, research organizations, and enterprises can develop new services without duplicating foundational infrastructure.

Key components: Digital services · Innovation platforms · Research collaboration · Developer ecosystem · Future agricultural applications

Guiding Principles

How the framework is designed

Infrastructure before applications

Build shared digital capabilities that support multiple solutions rather than isolated applications.

Interoperability by design

Enable diverse systems to communicate through open and standardized mechanisms.

Technology neutrality

Ensure flexibility by avoiding dependence on any specific technology or vendor.

Collaboration over centralization

Strengthen coordination while respecting the autonomy of participating institutions.

Evidence-based evolution

Continuously refine the framework through research, implementation, and evaluation.

Public value

Focus on creating long-term value for the agricultural ecosystem.

Chapter 9

PHAOS — Post-Harvest Agriculture Operating System

To demonstrate the practical implementation of the Agricultural Digital Infrastructure framework, DIA Technologies has developed the concept of PHAOS — a reference implementation of ADI for the post-harvest horticulture ecosystem.

PHAOS is designed to demonstrate how shared digital infrastructure can strengthen coordination, interoperability, and decision-making without replacing existing institutional systems. The proposed pilot in Jammu & Kashmir will use PHAOS as the operational platform through which the principles of ADI will be evaluated in a real-world environment.
