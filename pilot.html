Part IV · Pilot Proposal

Pilot Implementation at IGC Lassipora, Pulwama

A focused, evidence-based pilot of Agricultural Digital Infrastructure at one of Jammu & Kashmir's largest Controlled Atmosphere storage clusters — more than 70 operational CA units and growing.

Chapter 10

Proposed Pilot Project

DIA Technologies proposes the Industrial Growth Centre (IGC), Lassipora, District Pulwama, as the initial location for the pilot implementation. The area hosts a large concentration of CA cold storage facilities, creating a unique ecosystem of storage operators, logistics providers, traders, growers, and other stakeholders.

Why IGC Lassipora

One of the largest clusters of CA cold storage facilities in Jammu & Kashmir.
A well-established post-harvest horticulture ecosystem.
Significant movement and storage of high-value horticultural produce.
Multiple independent stakeholders — ideal for demonstrating ecosystem-wide coordination.
Existing physical infrastructure providing a foundation for complementary digital infrastructure.
Strong potential for measurable outcomes and scalability to other clusters.

Objectives of the Pilot

  • Demonstrate Agricultural Digital Infrastructure within a real-world horticulture ecosystem.
  • Improve coordination among participating stakeholders.
  • Enable secure and trusted information exchange.
  • Enhance operational visibility across post-harvest activities.
  • Support evidence-based planning and decision-making.
  • Generate practical insights for future policy and programme development.
  • Develop a scalable model for wider implementation across Jammu & Kashmir and other regions.

Implementation Approach

The pilot will be implemented in a phased manner, from planning through evaluation.

  1. Phase I — Planning & Stakeholder Consultation

    • Identification of pilot area
    • Stakeholder consultations
    • Operational assessment
    • Pilot design

  2. Phase II — System Configuration & Deployment

    • Configuration of the reference implementation
    • Integration with participating stakeholders where appropriate
    • Capacity building and user orientation

  3. Phase III — Pilot Operations

    • Live implementation
    • Operational monitoring
    • Continuous technical support
    • Periodic review with the Department

  4. Phase IV — Evaluation & Reporting

    • Assessment of pilot outcomes
    • Documentation of lessons learned
    • Recommendations for future expansion

Chapter 11

Expected Benefits of the Pilot

Rather than replacing existing systems, the pilot aims to strengthen collaboration among stakeholders across the entire ecosystem.

Department of Horticulture

  • Improved visibility across the post-harvest ecosystem
  • Stronger evidence-based planning and decision-making
  • Better coordination with ecosystem stakeholders
  • Support for monitoring and evaluation
  • Actionable insights for future programmes and policy
  • A scalable digital framework for future expansion

Farmers

  • Improved access to coordinated post-harvest services
  • Enhanced information flow across the value chain
  • Reduced communication gaps between stakeholders
  • More efficient movement of produce through the system
  • Greater confidence through transparency and traceability

Cold Storage Operators

  • Improved operational visibility
  • Better coordination with ecosystem partners
  • Digital record management
  • Operational dashboards and analytical insights
  • Interoperable digital workflows without disrupting operations

Wider Ecosystem

Transporters, traders, exporters, research institutions, and financial institutions benefit through:

  • Improved coordination and faster information exchange
  • Reduced information fragmentation
  • Enhanced interoperability between organizations

Chapter 12

Pilot Implementation Methodology

A phased, collaborative, evidence-based approach, designed to minimize operational disruption and generate measurable outcomes.

  1. Phase I — Project Initiation & Planning

    • Approval of the pilot by the Department of Horticulture
    • Constitution of a Joint Coordination Committee
    • Identification of participating stakeholders
    • Detailed operational assessment
    • Finalization of pilot scope & implementation plan

  2. Phase II — Stakeholder Engagement

    • Consultations with cold storage operators
    • Meetings with growers and FPOs
    • Engagement with transporters and logistics partners
    • Coordination with research institutions and government agencies
    • Identification of existing operational workflows

  3. Phase III — Digital Infrastructure Deployment

    • System configuration and user onboarding
    • Integration with participating operational workflows where appropriate
    • Training, orientation, and technical support

  4. Phase IV — Pilot Operations

    • Continuous operational monitoring and stakeholder support
    • Collection of implementation feedback
    • Observation of coordination mechanisms
    • Periodic review meetings with the Department

  5. Phase V — Evaluation & Reporting

    • Assessment of objectives, participation, and effectiveness
    • Documentation of lessons learned
    • A final Pilot Completion Report submitted to the Department
Guiding Principles

Collaboration before centralization · Infrastructure before standalone applications · Evidence-based implementation · Minimal disruption to existing operations · Continuous stakeholder engagement · Transparency and accountability · Scalability and long-term sustainability.

Chapter 13

Monitoring, Evaluation & Success Metrics

A structured framework to assess implementation progress, stakeholder participation, operational performance, and overall outcomes.

Institutional

  • Number of participating organizations
  • Stakeholder engagement
  • Level of institutional collaboration
  • Frequency of coordinated activities

Operational

  • Improvement in information availability
  • Reduction in information fragmentation
  • Operational visibility
  • Timeliness of information exchange

Governance

  • Quality of stakeholder coordination
  • Effectiveness of review mechanisms
  • Responsiveness to challenges
  • Transparency of operations

Knowledge

  • Documentation of experiences
  • Identification of best practices
  • Lessons learned
  • Recommendations for policy

The pilot will be considered successful if it demonstrates that shared digital infrastructure can improve coordination, enhance trusted information exchange, support evidence-based decision-making, complement existing infrastructure, and generate a practical, scalable model for future implementation.

Chapter 14

Long-Term Roadmap

The pilot is envisioned as the first step in a long-term journey toward strengthening the digital foundations of the horticulture ecosystem.

Phase I — Pilot Demonstration

Implementation at IGC Lassipora, Pulwama, in collaboration with the Department of Horticulture and participating stakeholders.

Phase II — Expansion Across J&K

Progressive extension to other CA storage clusters, cold storage facilities, pack houses, grading centres, logistics networks, and wholesale markets.

Phase III — Statewide Digital Ecosystem

An integrated digital ecosystem connecting major post-harvest stakeholders while respecting institutional independence.

Chapter 15

Why This Pilot Matters for Jammu & Kashmir

Jammu & Kashmir offers an ideal environment for demonstrating Agricultural Digital Infrastructure — a nationally significant horticulture sector, strong institutional support, a diverse network of stakeholders, and strong potential for innovation, replication, and scalability.

Establish Jammu & Kashmir as a pioneer in Agricultural Digital Infrastructure.
Create a validated model for future implementation across other clusters.
Support future policy development through practical implementation evidence.
Strengthen the digital readiness of the horticulture sector.
Complement existing investments in physical infrastructure.
Contribute to the long-term modernization of India's post-harvest ecosystem.

Chapter 16 · Request for Approval

DIA Technologies respectfully requests the Department to grant approval in principle, nominate a nodal officer for coordination, facilitate stakeholder engagement, provide guidance through planning and evaluation, and review outcomes for future expansion.
